## What this guide is \{#what-it-is}

A free, plain-English guide to achondroplasia for families and for people living with it, from the first day after a diagnosis through adulthood. It brings together what the published clinical guidelines, the peer-reviewed research, and the dwarfism community already know, so that it is all in one place.

It is **educational, not medical advice**. It cannot replace clinicians who know you or your child, ideally a team experienced with skeletal dysplasias. Use it to understand what is going on, to prepare questions, and to recognise what good care looks like.

## How pages are written \{#how-written}

- **Guidelines first.** Recommendations come from the major published guidance: the International Consensus Statement (2022), the American Academy of Pediatrics clinical report (2020), the European Achondroplasia Forum papers, the 2026 UK consensus guidelines, and GeneReviews. The [Medical Library](/medical-library) lists and explains each one.
- **Every page lists its sources.** Numbered sources sit at the end of each page, with links to the original papers wherever they are freely available.
- **Disagreements are shown, not smoothed over.** When the guidelines differ, for example on when to do a first MRI or how often to repeat sleep studies, the page says so and names each source, because your own team may follow any of them. The [care checklist](/checklist) has a side-by-side comparison.
- **Evidence is labelled.** Results from company press releases or conference presentations are marked as company-reported or not yet peer-reviewed. Comparisons between drugs that come from separate trials are labelled as such, because no completed trial has compared them head to head.
- **Respectful language.** We follow community guidance from Little People of America and similar organisations. See the note on language in the [glossary](/glossary).

## How pages are checked \{#how-checked}

- **Review dates.** Every page shows when its medical content was last checked against its sources.
- **Automatic checks on every update.** Before the site is published, a build check confirms that every PubMed and PubMed Central citation links to the paper it describes. It does this by fetching each paper's real title and comparing it with the citation. Every link and section anchor inside the site is also checked. Off-site links are checked every week.
- **Accessibility.** Pages are tested with automated accessibility tools, in both light and dark mode, and work without JavaScript.
- **Full reviews.** In October 2026 every page was re-checked against the current guidelines and literature. The corrections are listed on [What's new](/updates).

## Fast-moving topics \{#fast-moving}

Drug approvals, prices, trial results, and government benefits change often. Pages that cover them give an "as of" date. Before acting on a status, check it with your care team or the official source. That means the regulator in your country, the drug's prescribing information, or the benefits agency.

## Corrections \{#corrections}

If you spot an error, something out of date, or something unclear, please [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/achondroplasia/achondroplasia.github.io/issues) or suggest an edit directly. The full text of the guide is public. Please include a published source for any medical claim. Safety-relevant corrections are prioritised.

## Independence and privacy \{#independence}

- The guide is free and carries no advertising.
- It does not use analytics, tracking, or cookies. Fonts are served from this site, and search runs entirely in your browser.
- The content is published under a [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). You may share and adapt it with credit, but it must not be presented as medical advice.
