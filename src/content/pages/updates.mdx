## 10 October 2026: diagrams and growth charts \{#2026-10-diagrams}

The guide now has its first diagrams and charts. Each one states its main point in a caption, names its source, and has a text version for screen readers and for anyone who prefers words.

- **Achondroplasia growth charts.** Height, head size and weight-for-height for boys and girls, redrawn from the CLARITY study's published 5th–95th percentile curves (Hoover-Fong et al., 2021), with the numbers in a table underneath. See [Childhood](/childhood#growth), [First years](/first-years#big-head) and [Nutrition](/nutrition#bmi-problem).
- **How the growth brake works.** A diagram of FGFR3 stuck on in a growth-plate cell, and of how CNP eases it. On [Treatments](/treatments#how-the-medicines-work), it also shows where each growth medicine acts. See [Understanding it](/understanding#fgfr3).
- **Inheritance chances.** Pictures of 100 pregnancies for each kind of couple, and of how many children are born to average-height parents. See [Understanding it](/understanding#inheritance).

## 5 October 2026: full content review \{#2026-10-review}

Every page was re-checked against the current guidelines and literature, and every PubMed citation was matched against the paper it links to. Each page now shows a review date of 5 October 2026.

### Corrections

- **Citations.** Fifteen links to PubMed pointed to unrelated papers, and a few cited papers could not be found at all. These have been replaced with verified sources, and every claim that relied on them was re-checked. Some were softened, and a few were removed because no source supports them. An automatic check now runs before every update to stop this happening again (see [how pages are checked](/about#how-checked)).
- **Urgency advice made consistent.** The same symptom now gets the same advice on every page, using the three tiers on [Warning signs](/warning-signs): emergency now, call the same day, or raise at the next visit. For example, new weakness or a change in walking in a child is a same-day call, not "same week".
- **ER card.** It now notes that both vosoritide and navepegritide can cause a short-lived drop in blood pressure, that staff should record the time of the last dose, and that the prescriber should be asked about pausing treatment before surgery.
- **Vosoritide in the UK.** It is *not* licensed in Great Britain, and NICE has suspended its appraisal. An earlier version wrongly said it was approved and funded. In Northern Ireland, the EU licence applies. See [Global resources](/international).
- **Adult health figures.** Several numbers had been attributed to the wrong study. The body-composition finding was reversed: compared with average-height adults of the same BMI, adults with achondroplasia had *less* internal fat and *lower* blood pressure. The spinal-stenosis figures had been taken from back-pain data. A study of 25 adults had been described as 437. See [Adults & aging](/adults) and [Nutrition & exercise](/nutrition).
- **Pain medicines.** The page now carries the FDA's breathing-risk warning for gabapentin and pregabalin, which matters because sleep apnea is common in adults with achondroplasia. It also gives a more realistic picture of what steroid injections achieve for spinal stenosis. See [Pain management](/pain).
- **Eye care.** No major guideline recommends routine specialist eye checks for achondroplasia. An earlier version suggested otherwise. See [Vision](/vision).
- **School rights (US).** The IEP and 504 plan comparison has been corrected; a 504 plan *does* require an evaluation. See [Learning & school](/learning).
- **Organizations and clinics.** Names and links have been corrected for patient organizations in about a dozen countries. A closed Paris hospital has been removed, and the International Skeletal Dysplasia Registry is now listed at UCLA. See [Global resources](/international).
- **The International Consensus Statement has 136 recommendations**, not 160.

### Updates

- **Medicines (as of 5 October 2026).** The FDA is due to decide by 28 February 2027 on converting vosoritide to full approval. Vosoritide's phase 3 trial in hypochondroplasia was positive and has been filed with the FDA. Navepegritide's EU decision is expected in late 2026. Infigratinib's FDA application was announced in August 2026, and its phase 3 results are now published. TYRA-300's first results are expected in early 2027. The first head-to-head trial, of BMN 333 against vosoritide, began in 2026. See [Treatments](/treatments).
- **New UK guidelines (2026)** for children and young people are now reflected throughout. They recommend an MRI of the brain and spine within 3 months and a sleep study by 6 months. A new table on the [care checklist](/checklist#guidelines-differ) shows where the AAP, International Consensus, European Achondroplasia Forum, and UK guidelines differ.
- **Immunizations.** Rewritten for the current US situation, where the federal schedule is being challenged in court and the American Academy of Pediatrics publishes its own. There is a new by-country summary for the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and the EU. See [Immunizations](/immunizations#by-country).
- **Benefits and rights.** Updated for 2026 in the US, UK (including Scotland), EU, Canada, Australia, and Japan. See [Careers & work](/career) and [Global resources](/international).
- **Glossary and Medical Library.** About 15 new glossary terms, including accelerated vs. full approval, the AFMS score, and NDA/PDUFA. There are 8 new Medical Library entries, including the hypochondroplasia diagnosis consensus, the CLARITY growth references, and the navepegritide and infigratinib trial papers.
- **New page:** [About & editorial policy](/about), which explains how the guide is written, sourced, checked, and corrected.
